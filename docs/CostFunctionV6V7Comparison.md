# Cost function: v6.4 versus v7.1, side by side

Status: written 2026-09 on branch `michaz/typical-durations` from the release tags `v6.4`
(= `release/v6`) and `v7.1` / `v7.1.1`, the code each run class installs, the estimation
artifacts in `input/v7.0/mode_params_estimated.yaml`, and Rakow (2026), chapters 4 and 9.
Companion document: `CostFunctionReestimationAudit.md`, which explains what the
re-estimation on `michaz/choicemodel` does with the findings below.

## Summary

v6.4 and v7.1 differ in three layers at once: the money and time coefficients, the
mode-specific time terms, and the noise structure. v7 was built to fix one observed
symptom, an implausibly large bike-speed response in v6.4. It fixed that mainly by adding a
large amount of frozen per-person heterogeneity and switching to a deterministic selector,
not by changing the bike lever itself, which is essentially unchanged. That same
heterogeneity, combined with a money lever that is 2.5 to 9 times weaker in utils, is what
removed the price response. v7.1 is better on the layers it was validated on, share by
distance and mode users per day, and worse or unmeasured on price derivatives. In the
released v7.0 and v7.1 configs ride was not even choosable.

A note on v6.4's code path: its config carries an empty `advancedScoring` block, which does
activate the experimental scoring module built in June 2024 for the coming taste variations
(`ConfigUtils.hasModule` is true for any module the XML declares). With the defaults it
reduces exactly to the classical function with income exponent 1 and no random terms. The
v7 machinery was thus already inside v6.3 and v6.4, shipped inert.

## How each was derived

**v6.4 is an inherited specification, not an estimate.** Performing is the old VSP value of
6 utils/h inflated from 2017 to 2022 prices, which at 1 util/EUR is a value-of-time
assumption of about 6 EUR per hour. Fuel and daily costs come from Martins-Turner et al.
(2024). The car daily lump was tuned by hand from 3 EUR in v6.0 through 4 EUR to 5 EUR in
v6.2 (commit `1b769010`) because, as the config comment says, the full ownership cost gave
too unattractive a car mode. Ride is derived from car with a detour factor of one through
`RideScoringParamsFromCarParams`, giving double time and equal distance cost. The four
constants were calibrated to SrV shares by the outer-loop ASC calibrator. Exploratory
Biogeme scripts existed in the v6.x branch from January 2025, but nothing estimated was
shipped.

**v7.1 is a two-stage estimate transferred into the simulation.** The choice data were
generated by `src/main/sh/computeChoices.sh` in the v6.4 environment: v6.4 config, 10pct
plans, one iteration for travel times, reference persons only.

- Stage 1, trip level, multinomial logit on 27,732 SrV trips
  (`estimate_biogeme_trip_choice.py`). Estimated: performing, marginal utility of money,
  income exponent, one fixed-cost perception factor shared by car and pt, four constants.
  Daily costs were prorated per trip by beeline-distance share (`dist_weight`). Ride detour
  fixed at one, transfers fixed at -1. Results: 5.501, 0.397, 0.276, 0.269. This is table
  4.2 of the thesis and the header of `mode_params_estimated.yaml`.
- Stage 2, plan level, mixed logit on 8,400 persons with 70 candidate plans each
  (`estimate_biogeme_plan_choice.py`), the candidates generated by replaying the
  SubtourModeChoice mutation on the observed plan. All stage-1 parameters held fixed.
  Estimated: constant means and normal standard deviations for car, pt, bike, ride, extra
  time disutility for bike and ride, and the bus-leg constant. The thesis states that a
  joint estimation failed from overparameterization and correlation in the cost terms.
- Transfer: the per-person draws were produced inside the calibration runs by the
  experimental scoring factory (`IndividualPersonScoringParameters`) and stamped into the
  population as the `modeTasteVariations` attribute, which is how the released population
  carries them. The final run reads them back through the core `tasteVariations`
  parameter set. The trip-level normal term is scaled as the plan-level Gumbel variance
  divided by 3.32 trips, with the variance (0.495) passed where a standard deviation is
  expected. BestScore replaces ChangeExpBeta because the noise is now explicit. Constant
  means were then recalibrated to shares, separately per sample.

Two identification notes from the estimation output itself. In stage 1 the money coefficient
and the perception factor are correlated at -0.79 (robust); in stage 2 the car and ride
standard deviations are correlated with their means at -0.92 and -0.90. Finding 1 of the
audit explains why: there is no price variation in the sample, so the money, perception and
time split is fixed by functional form.

## What each version has and does not have

**The bike-speed fix.** The thesis's baseline experiment on v6.4 (section 4.1.3, 3pct, 12
runs) gives bike share 17.3% to 27.2% for +2 km/h, a 58% increase, against 27.1% from Li
et al. (2018a). With the v7 model the same experiment gives 17.4% to 23.1%, a 33% increase.
But the bike time coefficient is 6.88 in v6.4 and 6.81 in v7.1: a 3 km bike trip gains the
same 0.29 utils from +2 km/h in both versions. The entire change in response comes from
what surrounds that shift. In v6.4 the only noise is the scale-1 logit of ChangeExpBeta,
and the thesis shows the plan memories collapse so that realized choices are near
deterministic. In v7.1 a person with four bike trips against four pt trips carries a
frozen taste difference with standard deviation 7.8 utils, plus trip noise. A 0.29 utils
per trip shift moves few agents across such a margin. Damping is the mechanism, and
damping applies to every lever equally.

**The price collapse.** The price levers shrank in utils by the ratios above, and the same
frozen heterogeneity damps them further. The in-loop monitor measures v7.1 at pt fare
-0.02 to -0.05 and car cost at most -0.16 open loop, against literature -0.3 and -0.3 to
-0.4. Neither the thesis nor the v6 release ever measured a price elasticity. For v6.4 the
monitor's baseline cell is still pending, but by construction its response must be several
times larger than v7.1's, and the thesis's own argument implies it over-responds to any
systematic shift, price included. So neither version is known to have a defensible price
derivative.

**The daily car cost was patched in both versions.** v6.4 carried 35% of the full
ownership cost by hand tuning. v7.1 carries 27% through a named and estimated parameter.
In euros they are nearly the same; in utils v7.1 is a third of v6.4 because the money
coefficient dropped. (The v7.1 config comment says 14.90 EUR, but the value, the yaml and
the thesis all use 14.30 EUR.)

**Ride.** SrV shows the ride share peaking at about 3.5 km. v6.4 reproduces a peak near
5 km, but by accident: its ride constant is the most negative of the four, which
suppresses short trips because a per-trip constant dominates the score when the time term
is small, and its doubled car time plus car distance cost suppresses long trips. The peak
is the crossover of two penalties, not a modeled mechanism; nothing in v6.4 knows about
school runs or household drivers, which is why the thesis calls it "right for the wrong
reasons" (section 4.3.3). v7.1 replaced the doubled time by an estimated ride effort of
4.44 utils/h on top of performing and gave ride the widest taste spread, 2.86. With a
per-person offset that large, ride usage becomes nearly independent of distance and
purpose, the peak flattens, and ride F1 falls from 0.20 to 0.08. On top of that, from the
May 2025 commit that entered the estimated parameters (`c1cd5a15`) until v7.1.1 in June
2026 (`0537c8b5`, issue #164), the shipped configs left ride out of the SubtourModeChoice
mode set. With ride outside the set, a subtour containing a ride trip is skipped entirely
(`containsUnknownMode`) and the single-trip branch only touches pt and walk legs, so ride
trips are frozen: never assigned, never removed. The published v7.0 10pct output ran that
way (its output config says `car,pt,bike,walk`), and its 5.80% ride share is simply the
share of the calibrated input plans. The ASC calibration itself was not affected: the
three published v7.1 calibration runs (`berlin-v7.1/calibrated-*pct/output`) have ride in
the mode set, 90% of agents tried ride at least once, ride converged to 5.92-5.93% against
the 5.96% target, and their output configs carry exactly the constants shipped in v7.1.
For v7.0 the same follows from the plans: between the pre-calibration and the calibrated
1pct plans, ride trips move in both directions for about 18% of matched persons. So the
constants are consistent with ride choosable, v7.1.1 restores the setup they were
calibrated in, and the defect is confined to runs made with the v7.0 and v7.1 configs as
shipped, where ride was a fixed block that no policy could move. The audit's
re-estimation keeps ride's person-level term as the one genuine taste term, interpreted as
household driver availability, and treats the missing purpose and distance structure as
the open modeling gap.

**Fit.** On the 9,745 reference trips the thesis reports log-likelihood -43,144 for v6.4
and -15,684 for v7, but accuracy 0.48 versus 0.44 and ride F1 0.20 versus 0.08. The
likelihood gain is largely the added noise removing overconfidence; the composition gain
in share by distance is real and is what the audit attributes to the effort terms, not to
the taste spreads (finding 4).

**Income.** v6.4 assumes exponent 1, so a person with double income has half the price
sensitivity. v7.1 estimates 0.28 from bracket-imputed income through functional form
alone. The audit's plan-level check (finding 3) finds no support for either value.

## Is v7.1 superior?

On composition, yes: mode-specific time terms give the distance slope a carrier that v6.4
lacked, and the bus and transfer terms are sensible. On derivatives, no version has been
shown right; v7.1 is known to be badly inelastic to price, v6.4 is presumed badly elastic
to everything. The honest reading is that v7 traded one visible symptom for an invisible
one. The re-specification on `michaz/choicemodel` keeps the part of v7 that carried the
composition gain, the effort terms, and drops the parts that did the damping. Its
bike-speed response is the open validation item the audit lists, with one concrete
anchor: the thesis's 33% is tied to a single infrastructure study, so the office still has
to decide what the target should be.

## Two recommendations

### a) v6: switch on the situational error term v6.4 already has wired, at an estimated scale

What v7 was buying was dispersion at the choice margin. Without it, agents with identical
utilities switch en masse, which is both the +58% bike-speed response and the step-like
share-by-distance curves the thesis shows. Finding 5 of the audit says that, mode by mode,
most of what v7 booked as permanent taste is situational noise. So the direct route for v6
is the situational container alone, and v6.4 already carries it: the empty
`advancedScoring` block activates the experimental module, whose per-trip random term is
inert only because its scale is zero.

The change is one config parameter, `pseudoRamdomScale`, set to an estimated standard
deviation. The distribution defaults to normal and the scale is used as a standard
deviation (`PseudoRandomScorer.sampleNormal`), so v7's units slip cannot recur. Keep
ChangeExpBeta: the estimation's residual plan-level Gumbel then comes from the selector at
scale 1, which is the normalization finding 6 asks for. Keep every money lever as it is.
Recalibrate the four constants, as any change requires.

Estimate the scale rather than guess it: `estimate_biogeme_plan_choice.py` with v6.4's
parameters held fixed and `--est-error-component` is an afternoon's work (the car daily
cost of 5 EUR needs a small edit, since `prepare.py` hardcodes 14.30). Reference points
from the audit, in units where performing is about 5.5:

| specification | error sd per trip |
|---|---|
| re-estimated utilities, k=9 | 2.2 |
| published v7 utilities plus error component, k=9 | 2.4 |

In v6.4's larger utility units expect a somewhat larger value. For a four-trip plan,
independent per-trip noise at sd 2.2 gives a bike-versus-pt dispersion of 6.2 utils,
against 7.8 from v7's tastes, so the bike-speed response should land in v7's region without
touching a single lever. Price response is damped only by the amount the data assign to
unobserved utility, from a level presumed too high. Validate with the finite-difference
bike-speed pair and the walk and bike shares by distance.

What this deliberately leaves out is permanent taste. The audit finds only ride's is
genuine, and adding it needs the population attribute pipeline, so it is a second step,
not this one.

### b) v7: drop the fixed-cost perception factor, daily lumps at full value

Ranking v7's additions by how indefensible they are times how much they cost in price
response, the perception factor wins clearly. It is a patched symptom (audit finding 2),
identified through a proration the audit calls bogus, and collinear with the money
coefficient at -0.79. Removing it multiplies the fare and fixed-cost levers by 3.7. The
alternatives lose on one of the two counts. The income exponent has no support, but it
redistributes sensitivity across incomes and barely moves aggregate elasticity. The taste
spreads are mislabeled, but they hold real dispersion, and deleting them without a
replacement recreates v6's overshoot. The money coefficient is a choice rather than an
estimate, but the thesis defends the implied 13.9 EUR/h with literature, while v6's
coefficient implies 6.9 EUR/h. BestScore is consistent given explicit noise, and the
apparatus effect the in-loop monitor measured is under 35%.

The change is two numbers expressing one decision: car `dailyMonetaryConstant` from -3.84
to -14.30 and pt from -0.806 to -3.0 (the `--price-perception 1.0` case of the audit's
minimal fix). Recalibrate the four constant means. The stored per-person draws stay as they
are.

| lever | v7.1 | after | v6.4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| car daily lump, utils | 1.53 | 5.68 | 5.0 |
| pt daily lump, utils | 0.32 | 1.19 | 3.0 |
| pt fare elasticity, open loop | -0.02 to -0.05 | about -0.07 to -0.19 | not measured |
| car cost elasticity, open loop | at most -0.16 | roughly -0.3 | not measured |

The projections are rough because the derivative at small changes scales with exposure,
and the lump lever only has mass at the mode-free-day margin. The fuel lever stays at
0.059 utils per km, 2.5 times weaker than v6's, because that comes from the money
coefficient, not from perception.

Two things to watch. First, the historical reason perception exists is that full lumps
under share-only calibration drove the car constant positive and concentrated car on
many-trip agents ("ASC harvesting"). That episode had a lump of about 14 utils. Here it is
5.7, and v6.4 calibrated a 5.0 lump to a constant of -0.49, so expect the car constant to
settle near -0.5 rather than cross zero. If it does cross, or if car share tilts toward
short trips, that is the signal for the audit's step 1, estimating car and pt time
efforts, not for bringing perception back. Second, the car taste spread of 1.51 partly
proxies the ownership margin the full lump now carries, so expect some residual
over-damping of the car response until the spreads are re-estimated.
